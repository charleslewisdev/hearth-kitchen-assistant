# Cooking

## Finding a recipe

The **Recipes** tab opens your **Recipe Book**. On a computer, **Recipe Book** and **Tags** sit
under **Recipes** on the left; on a phone they are two tabs at the top of the screen.

Each card shows the title, the recipe types (breakfast, dinner, side, sauce and so on), up to four
tags as coloured chips, and along the bottom the servings on the left and the total time (prep plus
cook) on the right, with your ★ rating between them. A recipe you've logged also says how often
and how lately it was cooked.

- **Sort** (next to the recipe count): **Newest**, **Recently edited**, **A–Z**, **Quickest**,
  **Top rated**, **Recently cooked** or **Most cooked**. While you search, the first choice becomes
  **Best match**. Recipes with no times, no rating or no cooks go last under those sorts.
- The two buttons beside it switch between **cards** and a compact **list**. Hearth remembers your
  sort and view on each device.
- **Search** matches words in recipe titles and tags. It starts matching as you type, so `chick`
  finds "Chicken Soup".
- While you type, Hearth may suggest **contains _food_** (recipes that use that ingredient) and
  **tagged _tag_**. Tap one to add it as a filter.
- The chips under the search box filter by recipe type and by your most used tags. **Filters**
  opens the full list of tags.
- Active filters show as chips with a cross. Tap the cross to remove one.

## The recipe screen

A recipe shows its title, recipe types and tags, prep and cook times and notes if you added them,
and a **Source** line: the source name you typed and, if there is one, a link to the web page it
came from (it opens in a new tab). **Edit** opens the recipe form, where you can add or fix the
link.

Switch between **Ingredients** and **Steps** with the two buttons under the servings.

### Scaling servings

Use **-** and **+** next to **Servings** to scale the recipe. Every ingredient amount changes to
match. For example, a recipe for 4 set to 6 multiplies each amount by 1.5.

- Amounts show as kitchen fractions where they are close to one (`¼`, `⅓`, `1 ½`). Other values
  show with up to two decimals.
- Grams, millilitres and litres always show as decimals, for example `187.5 g`.
- Lines with no amount, such as "salt, to taste", do not change.
- Scaling is straight multiplication. It does not adjust for things like baking times or pan sizes.
- The change is not saved. When you come back to the recipe it shows its own servings again.

The servings you choose also apply to **Add to list** and **Start cooking**.

### US or metric

When a recipe prints an amount both ways, such as "3 cups (360g) flour" or "1 cup (5 oz/158 g)
flour", a **US | Metric** switch appears above the ingredients. Pick **Metric** to read the grams
and millilitres the book gave, for weighing on a scale.

- Only amounts the recipe printed switch. Hearth does not convert cups to grams itself, because
  that depends on the food. A line with one measure shows it either way.
- The second amount scales with the servings like the first.
- Your choice is remembered on this device, and cook mode uses it too. Its **Ingredients** panel
  has its own **Metric** / **US** button.
- The shopping list always uses the first amount.

### Sending a recipe to someone

To send a recipe to someone who is not in your Hearth, such as a relative who asked for it, use
the buttons at the top of the recipe.

- **Share** opens your phone's share menu, so you can send the recipe as a text message, an email
  or a chat message. On a computer without a share menu, it copies the recipe instead; paste it
  wherever you like.
- **Print** prints the recipe on one or two pages: title, photo, every ingredient and every step.
  To send it as a file, choose **Save as PDF** in the print window.

Both send the recipe as you have it on screen, so set the servings and US or metric first. When
a line is made from another of your recipes, such as tortillas in a taco recipe, that recipe is
added at the end, at its own servings. Only those are added: a recipe that one is made from in
turn is not. The person you send it to gets a copy. They do not need a Hearth account, and
nothing is shared from your Hearth.

## Rating and the cook log

Under the title, tap a star to give the recipe your household's rating; tap the same star again
to clear it. Once you have logged a few cooks with ratings, **Recent cooks: 3 → 4 → 5** beside
the stars shows how they went, oldest to newest, which helps while you are dialling a recipe in.
The rating is yours to set: Hearth does not average the log into it.

**Cooked it**, under the steps, is the recipe's history. **Log a cook** records:

- the day (today unless you change it) and how many it served,
- how it went, one to five stars,
- notes for next time, such as "less salt, double the gremolata",
- photos of it, which also join the recipe's photos.

Each entry says who logged it. **Edit** and **Delete** are on every entry, for anyone in the
household. Deleting an entry keeps its photos in the recipe's gallery.

## Photos

Under a recipe's steps, **+ Add photos** adds photos from your phone's camera or gallery. Hearth
shrinks each one before it leaves the phone and removes its location and camera details.

- The first photo becomes the **cover**: it shows at the top of the recipe and on its card.
- Tap a photo to see it full size. **Make cover** changes the cover, and **Delete** removes the
  photo after asking. Arrows (or the arrow keys) step through them.
- A recipe imported from photos or a PDF keeps what it was read from under **Source pages**, so
  you can check the recipe against the original. A PDF is a download link.
- Some computer browsers can't open iPhone HEIC photos. Add those from the phone, or save them
  as JPEG first.

## Tags

**Recipes → Tags** lists every tag with how many recipes carry it. Tap the count to see those
recipes. **Edit** on a tag lets you:

- **Rename** it. If another tag already has that name, Hearth offers to merge the two instead.
- Pick its **colour**, or **Auto** for the colour Hearth chose from its name.
- **Merge** it into another tag. Every recipe that had it gets the other one instead.
- **Delete** it. It comes off its recipes; the recipes stay.

**Remove N unused tags** at the top deletes every tag no recipe carries.

## Cook mode

Press **Start cooking** at the bottom of a recipe. Cook mode opens at the top, on step 1, or on the
step you were up to if you come back to a recipe part way through.

- **Tap a step** to mark it done. It is ticked, struck through and dimmed. Tap it again to undo.
- The first step not yet done is shown larger. The header says **Step N of M**.
- **Ingredients** at the top opens a panel with the ingredient list at the servings you chose.
  **Close** or tapping outside it closes it.
- **Back** returns to the recipe.

Your progress is kept on this device. If you reload the page, your phone closes the tab in the
background, or you go back to the recipe and return, cook mode picks up with the same steps ticked
and your timers still running. It is not shared: opening the recipe on another phone or computer
starts fresh. Once every step is done and no timers are left, cook mode forgets the recipe, so
next time starts over. Progress left untouched for a day is forgotten too. If the browser does not
let Hearth store anything (some private windows), cook mode still works but starts over on reload.

### The screen stays on

Cook mode asks the browser to keep the screen from dimming or locking. This works in most
current browsers on phones and computers, as long as Hearth is opened over `https://`. It does not
work in Firefox. If the browser refuses, the screen dims as usual; nothing else changes.

If you switch to another app and come back, cook mode asks again.

### Timers

Times written in a step, such as "simmer 20 minutes", appear as buttons. Tap one to start a timer.
[Adding recipes by hand](adding-recipes.md#timers-in-step-text) lists what counts as a time.

- Running timers sit in a tray at the bottom of the screen, labelled with the step and the time,
  for example "Step 2 · 20 minutes". The tray stays put while you scroll to later steps.
- You can run several timers at once.
- When a timer finishes, it shows **Done**, beeps three times, and vibrates on phones that
  support it. It repeats every 5 seconds for up to a minute, then stays on screen silently.
- **Stop** ends a timer, running or finished.

Timers keep counting if you leave cook mode or reload the page, and come back with the time that
is really left. A timer that finished while you were away shows **Done** when you return. It
beeps only if it finished less than a minute ago, the same as a timer left ringing with the page
open; after that it stays on screen silently until you press **Stop**.

A timer keeps its time while you are away, but it only beeps from cook mode. **It will not ring
while you are on another page, and cannot be relied on to ring while you are in another app or the
screen is locked.** Keep cook mode open while something is in the oven. After a reload, the browser needs one tap on the page, anywhere, before it lets
Hearth make a sound again; ticking a step is enough. If your phone is set to silent, you may not
hear the beep.
